## 1. One-liner
Employees of mid-size German companies (50–1,000 staff) who suddenly have to organise care for a parent struggle with care-grade applications, benefits and finding services next to their job; we give them an AI-supported care navigator with a human care adviser, paid by the employer as a tax-free benefit, so that they keep working and the employer loses fewer hours. **Unproven:** no source in this brief shows that navigation help reduces absence or cut hours (section 2).

Wedge chosen for this brief: **(ii) mid-size employers without a family-service contract**. Wedge (i), software sold to the bundlers, is not chosen: no evidence that any bundler buys third-party tooling (section 6).

## 2. Problem evidence
**Hard data**
- **How many employees:** 4.6m people of working age gave informal care in 2023; this could rise to ~6.4m by 2045. Care-related lost work is estimated at ~184,000 to 264,000 full-time equivalents in 2026, rising to ~229,000 to 327,000 by 2045 (two model variants; the study's summary quotes the lower one) [B] ([Bertelsmann Stiftung, 2026](https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/fileadmin/files/user_upload/BSt_Informelle_Pflege_und_Erwerbstaetigkeit.pdf)).
- 4.1m carers were employed in 2021; "deutlich mehr als 80 Prozent" of them worked in establishments with ≥20 staff [B] ([Fraunhofer FIT, Nov 2023](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/b55b85da-c6a4-4425-be2f-f746eb91a83e/content); data 2021, >3 years old).
- Of carers aged 18 to under 50, ~75 % were employed in 2022, working 35 hours a week on average and caring ~3 hours per working day. About 625,000 cut their working hours when care began; 440,000 extended them [B] ([iwd, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.iwd.de/pdf/doppelte-herausforderung-durch-beruf-und-pflege-669794/); data: SOEP 2022, >3 years old).
- "acht Prozent aller Erwerbstätigen" care next to work, i.e. ~80 per 1,000 staff; the typical caring employee is ~51 years old with 17 years in the company [B] (NRW labour ministry figures, quoted in [Personalwirtschaft](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=201653)).
- 46.9 % of employed carers are highly burdened; 45.2 % of part-timers cut hours because of care [B] (WIdO 2026, see `research/pain-patients.md` P3).
- **The navigation gap:** only "3 Prozent der Anspruchsberechtigten" use statutory care counselling [A] (GKV-SV § 7a report, see `research/pain-patients.md` P1). Counter-evidence: WIdO finds "fehlender Bedarf" and preference are the main reasons for not using services [B] (same file).
- **The employer gap:** 79.5 % of companies say family-friendly measures matter for supporting caring employees (2023: 64.8 %). But only 15.7 % offer help finding care services or cooperate with care providers (2015: 14.0 %), and 2.4 % train in-house "Pflegelotsen" [B] ([IW Unternehmensmonitor Familienfreundlichkeit 2026, table A-3](https://www.bmbfsfj.bund.de/resource/blob/293882/3408f56948ed9e772410b65ea57f9dec/unternehmensmonitor-familienfreundlichkeit-2026-data.pdf); 1,005 companies with ≥5 staff, Feb–May 2026). *Inference:* stated importance rose 15 points in three years, the offer barely moved in eleven (14.0 % → 15.7 %). The survey alone cannot tell an open market from weak willingness to pay. The rest of this brief can, and it points to **weak willingness to pay**:
  - pme, famPlus, awo lifebalance, voiio and heycare sold to employers during those years (section 6) and the offer rate moved 1.7 points;
  - the one eldercare-only vendor with a public price (Eldercare24, €299–499 a month) shows no customer;
  - free employer-side routes exist and few firms take them (2.4 % train Pflegelotsen; NRW programme: 690 employers);
  - the only young entrant with named customers (heycare, 2022) entered with a bundle, childcare first.
  - What stays open: how many firms with 50–1,000 staff have no contract at all. No source; only outreach can count it.
- **Missing link to the payer (unproven):** the figures above are costs to carers and to the economy. Nothing here shows that help with applications lowers absence or cut hours for the employer. Hours are cut because of ~3 hours of care per working day, which a navigator does not remove (*inference*).

**Why now**
- Demographics: 5,161,367 care recipients at home with statutory care insurance ("ambulant gepflegte SPV-Versicherte") on 31 Dec 2025 [A] ([PNOG cabinet draft, reasoning to § 7d](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/3_Downloads/Gesetze_und_Verordnungen/GuV/P/Kabinett-GE_Pflegeneuordnungsgesetz_PNOG.pdf)).
- Rules change on 1 Jan 2027 if the PNOG passes: care-grade thresholds rise to 15 / 30 / 50 points for grades 1–3; the relief allowance ends for grade 1; Pflegegeld becomes an "Entlastungsbudget" (€380 in grade 2, €630 in grade 3); decision deadlines are suspended from 1 Jan to 30 Apr 2027 [A] (same draft, § 15, § 37, § 142b). **Bill, may change:** first reading 5 Nov 2026 (BT-Drs. 21/8869); the minister announced changes to the relief allowance and respite care [B] ([Bundestag](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw45-de-pflegeneuordnungsgesetz-1225076), [AOK G+G, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.aok.de/pp/gg/update/erste-lesung-pnog/)). *Inference:* more confusion and more rejected applications in 2027 raise demand for help.
- Against "why now": the same bill builds the free substitute (section 8).

## 3. Customer
- **User:** employee with a parent or partner who needs care (typical caring employee: ~51 years old, section 2).
- **Buyer / signer:** HR or benefits lead, in mid-size firms often the managing director. Works council review is possible where one exists (*inference*, section 8).
- **Payer:** the employer. The payment is tax-free for the employee: § 3 Nr. 34a lit. a EStG exempts employer payments "an ein Dienstleistungsunternehmen, das den Arbeitnehmer hinsichtlich der Betreuung von Kindern oder pflegebedürftigen Angehörigen berät oder hierfür Betreuungspersonen vermittelt" [A] ([§ 3 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__3.html)). *My reading:* the €600 cap in the text belongs to lit. b (short-term emergency care), not to counselling; to be confirmed by a tax adviser.
- **How large employers buy today [A] (TED awards, API fields opened):** as multi-year family-service or EAP frameworks, mostly bundles, with 1–2 bidders.

## 4. Solution & product wedge
- **Smallest product:** a web app plus a named human adviser.
  - Intake chat that builds a personal checklist ("Pflegefall in 48 hours": care-grade application, power of attorney, relief services, short-term care).
  - Pre-filled application forms and reminders; explanation of the MD assessment report.
  - A 30-minute call with a qualified care adviser when the case is not standard.
  - For HR: a one-page guide on Pflegezeit and Familienpflegezeit, two webinars a year. No usage report by person. With 1–2 cases per firm a year (section 7), even a count is a report by person, so HR gets no usage numbers at all. Renewal then has to rest on something else; this is unsolved (section 12).
- **What AI does:** answers from a curated, dated knowledge base of SGB XI rules and local services; drafts forms and letters; the adviser reviews anything sent to an insurer.
- **Non-goals:** no medical or nursing advice; no legal representation or objections (section 8); no placement of care workers; no childcare, EAP or mental health; no selling to Pflegekassen in year 1.
- **Honest limit:** every element above already exists at famPlus, pme, heycare (employer side) and Nui Care (app with AI, insurer side). Nui announced a "Pflege-Kompass" in Feb 2026 that "is intended to analyze care reports" of the MD; live status not shown [B] ([heise, 17 Feb 2026](https://heise.de/-11180202)). On 12 Nov 2026 Nui and AOK Bayern presented a "KI-Pflegegradassistent" (orientation on the likely care grade, a different feature) [B] ([nui.care](https://www.nui.care/), news item). The only differences left are price, speed of set-up and focus on firms the bundlers do not court. *Inference;* that such firms exist in number is not shown: heycare ("Über 450 Unternehmen") and voiio ("1000+") cannot all be large firms, and the Familienpakt channel in section 9 is the incumbents' channel too.

## 7. Business model & pricing
- **Model:** annual subscription per company, tiered by headcount; human adviser time included up to a cap.
- **Price hypothesis (inference from the benchmarks in section 5):** €300–500 per month for 50–250 staff. Above that the only benchmark is €4–6 per employee per year (SWR; one buyer's estimate, no award), i.e. €4,000–6,000 a year at 1,000 staff, no more than the small-firm list price. A higher per-head price (the first draft said €6–12) has no evidence. Test whether a per-case fee (employer pays only when an employee enrols; Wellthy's public plan works this way) sells better.
- **Unit economics, rough (inference, no source):** at 8 % carers and perhaps one in ten of them opening a case per year, a 300-person firm produces 2–3 cases a year. *My math:* 8 % × 1/10 = **0.8 cases per 100 staff per year = 0.4 per 100 in six months**, i.e. 1–2 cases per 300-person firm in a 6-month pilot. The one-in-ten rate is my guess; no German source for the use of employer-paid eldercare support is in this brief. This is the one usage estimate; sections 9, 12 and 13 use it. Revenue €3,600–6,000 then covers ample adviser time. The constraint is sales cost per small contract, not delivery cost.
- **Footnote (L03):** Pflegekassen may commission online carer courses under § 45 SGB XI (`research/scoring.md`). Not a second payer in this brief (red team R4). The PNOG folds home training under § 45 into the Pflegebegleitung budget (section 8).

## 8. Regulation & data
- **Medical device:** not intended. The product gives administrative and benefits guidance and has no medical purpose. *Inference;* the MDR text was not re-opened this run `[UNVERIFIED]`. Keep symptom, medication and care-technique advice out.
- **Legal services (RDG) [A]:** "Rechtsdienstleistung ist jede Tätigkeit in konkreten fremden Angelegenheiten, sobald sie eine rechtliche Prüfung des Einzelfalls erfordert" ([§ 2 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__2.html)). Independent out-of-court legal services are not allowed unless permitted ([§ 3 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__3.html)); § 5 allows them only as an ancillary service ([§ 5 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__5.html)). *Inference:* explaining benefits and filling in applications is likely fine; drafting objections against care-grade decisions is the risk zone. Needs one legal opinion (est. €2,000–4,000, my estimate) before launch. Nui lists Pflegewächter as a partner for this part [B] (Munich Startup, above).
- **Data protection [A]:** health data of relatives is a special category (Art. 9 GDPR); large-scale processing triggers a data-protection impact assessment (Art. 35(3)(b)) and a data-protection officer if it is the core activity (Art. 37(1)(c)) ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/DE/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)). The relative is a third party who must consent or be represented. Design: no personal data to the employer; EU hosting.
- **Works council [A]:** co-determination for "technische Einrichtungen, die dazu bestimmt sind, das Verhalten oder die Leistung der Arbeitnehmer zu überwachen" ([§ 87 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html)). Whether a benefit app without reporting falls under it is `[UNVERIFIED]`. Eldercare24 avoids the issue: "Es erfolgen keine individuellen oder aggregierten Auswertungen".
- **DiGA / DiPA:** no route. DiPA excludes navigation-only apps (`research/reimbursement-routes.md`).
- **EU AI Act:** transparency duties for chatbots likely apply; not checked `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- **PNOG (bill, BT-Drs. 21/8869; cabinet draft 30 Sep 2026; may change) [A]** ([cabinet draft](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/3_Downloads/Gesetze_und_Verordnungen/GuV/P/Kabinett-GE_Pflegeneuordnungsgesetz_PNOG.pdf)):
  - **§ 7a new, "Pflege-Cockpit":** every Pflegekasse opens "einen einheitlichen digitalen Zugang" for each insured care recipient and authorised relatives: benefits per care grade, benefits drawn, filing applications and receipts with status, the assessment's recommendations, provider and price search (functions 1–7, "spätestens bis zum 1. Juli 2028"); information on counselling and training offers (functions 8–10) by 1 Jan 2030. Abs. 2: Kassen "sollen" also give insured people digital information **before** a care need is established, including the conditions for applying; no date is set for this.
  - **§ 7c new, "Pflegebegleitung":** "Ab dem 1. Januar 2029" care recipients at home and their carers are entitled to professional support. The Kasse must offer "einen konkreten Termin" at home right after the first care grade; tasks include a care plan, help with applications for aids, showing relief options, and case management across benefit systems. Phone or video on request. Pflegegeld recipients must call it up once a year.
  - **§ 7d new, money:** "146 Euro je pflegebedürftiger Person in häuslicher Pflege" per year, "rechnerisch rund 760 Millionen Euro". "Die Pflegekassen können die Pflegebegleitung auch ganz oder teilweise auf Dritte übertragen". The reasoning notes that some Kassen have already handed today's counselling to third parties. The GKV-SV recommends staff numbers and qualification by 30 Sep 2027; municipalities declare a takeover by 31 Dec 2027.
  - *Inference, as a substitute for use:* from 2028–29 the statutory system offers, free, most of what this product does once a care grade exists. Free statutory counselling already exists today and only 3 % use it (section 2), so "free" alone has not removed the need. New is that § 7c is proactive ("einen konkreten Termin") and yearly for Pflegegeld recipients; that could change uptake. What stays outside: the weeks before the first care grade (partly touched by § 7a Abs. 2), the job side (Pflegezeit, talking to the employer), and speed. That residual is a one-off episode of a few weeks per employee.
  - *Inference, as a sales objection:* the damage comes before 2029. An HR lead asked in 2027–28 to sign 24 months can answer "the Kasse does this free from 2029".
  - *Inference, as a new market:* **the €760m is the Kassen's delivery budget, not an addressable market.** It is €146 per care recipient for visits and case management by qualified staff. No source in this brief shows that any of it buys third-party software, or that a Kasse would hand delivery to a company founded in 2027. The bill's own reasoning says "Bereits heute haben manche Pflegekassen die bisherige Pflegeberatung (vollständig) auf Dritte übertragen": those third parties exist and hold the references. Municipalities may take the task over. Nui Care already works with AOK Bayern and DAK. The buyer is signer cluster C5 (Pflegekasse), the route `research/scoring.md` treats as tender with references plus small direct awards; L03 was scored "K2 unproven" on the same buyer. It is a different customer and a different company, and it depends on a bill with a €7.6bn financing gap [B] ([AOK G+G](https://www.aok.de/pp/gg/update/erste-lesung-pnog/)).
- **Certifications:** none mandatory found. famPlus advertises a DEKRA audit against DIN 77009-1; buyers may ask for it. Not checked.
- **Time and cost to launch (my estimate, no source or quote behind it):** 3–4 months; legal and privacy set-up €4,000–8,000.

## 9. Go-to-market
- **First 10 customers (inference):** owner-led or mid-size firms with 150–1,000 staff in the Munich area with no family-service contract. Reach them through:
  - the Familienpakt Bayern network (it already promotes Pflegelotsen trainings; that its members are interested is an *inference*, and two of the four listed training providers are famPlus and pme München, so this is the incumbents' channel too);
  - company health insurers (BKKs): a BKK concept for supporting caring employees was presented at BKK Innovativ (Miele BKK per the file name; the text does not name it) [B] ([PDF](https://www.bkk-dachverband.de/fileadmin/Artikelsystem/BKK_INNOVATIV/Vorstellung_Konzept_pflegende_Mitarbeitende_BKK_Innovativ_Miele_BKK.pdf));
  - benefit brokers and platforms as resellers (voiio marketplace, Aon): not verified as open to new providers.
- **Pilot design (revised):** 3 firms, 6 months, **paid** (≥€250 a month, cancellable); a free pilot with a signed intent does not test payment.
  - Expected use at the section 7 estimate: 0.4 cases per 100 staff in 6 months, so about 4 cases across three 300-person firms (*my math:* 900 × 0.004 = 3.6). The first draft's bar (≥3 % of staff, i.e. 9 cases per firm) was about 7 times that estimate and is dropped.
  - What a pilot of this size can show: whether firms pay, whether anyone opens a case, and whether a firm renews at list price. What it cannot show: the usage rate (it cannot tell 0.4 from 1.0 per 100) or any effect on absence.
  - Conversion test: renewal at list price. Measure cases opened, time to care-grade application, hours saved (self-report); none of it goes to HR by person or as a count (section 4).
- **Constraints:** the founder is in Lisbon until Sep 2027; mid-size HR sales are relationship sales (red team R2). 24-month contracts at €299 a month do not pay for field sales, so the channel must be inbound, partners or webinars.
- Public tenders are closed to a new company (references). They become possible only as a subcontractor or consortium member of a bundler.
