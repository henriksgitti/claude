**Bottom line for the CEO (researcher's view, you decide):**
- **The customer is real but small and leaves fast.** About **100,000** people in Germany paid for obesity drugs themselves at the end of 2024 (IQVIA [B]); no newer count was found. Drug costs are about €130 to just under €500 a month (Verbraucherzentrale, Mar 2026 [B]). Where patients pay themselves, fewer than 10 % are still on the drug after 12 months in some countries (review [A]); UK self-payers at Juniper stayed 183 days on average, and cost was the top reason to stop (38.7 %) (company-reported study [B]).
- **The "companion" job is already done, often for free, by the companies that sell the drug.** GoLighter (Wellster, a Munich company) gives its drug customers free nutrition counselling and, since 1 Sep 2026, the Weight Watchers GLP-1 programme at no extra cost. It also sells side-effect "care packages" for €19.99–€32.99 [B]. Weight Watchers sells its GLP-1 programme on its own for €45 a month at list price, or €11.25 a month on a 12-month deal [B]. People with a BMI of 30–40 can get a reimbursed weight-loss app on prescription (Oviva Direkt, zanadio) [B]. The German App Store lists at least 25 GLP-1 tracker apps; most were launched in 2025–26 and have fewer than 60 ratings [C].
- **The "after the shot" gap is narrower than the first draft said.** No drug platform was found with a programme for people who stop (GoLighter, Voy pages checked 2026-11-15; Juniper DE site blocked) [B]. But: Weight Watchers is open to anyone at €11.25/month and its US site tells leavers that members who "stayed engaged … after discontinuing their medication continued to see results" [B]; a pharmacy brand (formoline, Certmedica) markets "Gewicht halten nach der Abnehmspritze" with a free app and a ~€96 pill pack [B]; the GLP-1 forum has free articles on stopping [C]; a Munich hospital runs a programme that phases the drug out (ZEPmedi-doc) [B]; and staying on a **lower dose** kept most of the weight off in a 2026 trial [A]. One small site (nachderspritze.de) describes the same 12-week programme as this brief, for under €129, still on a waitlist [B].
- **No moat, and the founder's decisive gap is marketing, not product.** Anyone can build this programme; one person has already designed and published the same one (nachderspritze.de, not launched). What decides success is buying customers cheaply in a restricted health ad category; neither the CEO nor a named co-founder has that skill (sections 6, 11).
- **The arithmetic fails** (section 5, my math, corrected in C14). The product is a 12-week programme at €129, bought once. €1m revenue needs ~**7,750 new customers a year**, ~7.8 % of all 2024 self-payers every year. Marketing at the 39–46 % of revenue seen in filings [A] allows only **€50–59** to win each customer. No desk source shows that this is possible; the benchmark comes from companies with repeat purchases, which this product lacks.
- **Recommendation: drop the GLP-1 companion as a venture (C12 option d).** Even a passed test would show a small one-off programme business, not a seed-fundable company (Germany-only TAM €12.9m a year at one purchase per self-payer). If you still want to learn what a paying consumer costs (useful for the midlife-women theme too), the only test worth running is the **redesigned pre-order test** in section 13 (one theme, paid pre-orders with refund, pass ≥10 for €300). The waitlist test of the first draft is withdrawn: it would have measured general diet interest, not this segment. Options are in task C12.

## 1. One-liner
People in Germany who pay €130–500 a month for a weight-loss drug, and especially those who stop because of cost or side effects, struggle to keep muscle, eat enough protein on little appetite and keep the weight off; we offer a low-cost, AI-supported nutrition and strength-training programme (optionally with a claims-compliant protein product), sold directly to them, so that they keep more muscle and regain less weight. **Unproven:** no source shows that a programme *started at the time of stopping* reduces regain. The closest evidence: in a Danish RCT, people who had done supervised exercise *during* a year of liraglutide regained 6.0 kg less in the year after stopping than those on the drug alone (S-LiTE, [EClinicalMedicine 2024, PMID 38544798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38544798/) [A]; 109 attended the follow-up). Strength training and protein do preserve lean mass during weight loss (section 2). Because the buyer wants "regain less" and the product may not promise it (section 4), the core promise cannot be stated in ads.

Wedge chosen in this brief: **(b) "after the shot" maintenance** (people stopping or tapering the drug). Wedge (a), "companion during treatment", is described for comparison but not recommended: the drug platforms already bundle it free (section 6).

## 3. Customer
- **User = buyer = payer:** the self-paying patient. No insurer pays for this companion. A reimbursed alternative exists only for BMI 30–40 (DiGA, section 6).
- **First segment (wedge b):** adults in Germany who stopped or are tapering a self-paid GLP-1 drug, plus those who are about to stop because of cost. Size: not counted anywhere. *Inference from the sources above:* with ~100,000 self-payers and persistence well under 50 % at 12 months, tens of thousands stop each year. This is unproven; no DE persistence data for self-payers exists. *Inference (red team F1):* the bifg data show ~16 % stop after the first use; such people lost little and have little to "keep". The real target is smaller: people who lost a lot and then stopped. No count exists for that group.
- **Comparison segment (wedge a):** people currently on the drug. Most reach it through telemedicine platforms that bundle coaching (section 6), or through their own physician plus a pharmacy. How the self-payer pool splits between these two routes is unknown.
- **Decision process:** individual, online, impulse-to-considered; price anchor is the drug (€130–500/month) on one side and WW (€11.25–45/month) or free DiGA on the other.
- **Profile:** YouGov describes interested households as "eher jüngere Haushalte, bei denen das Thema Selbstoptimierung einen hohen Stellenwert hat" ([BVE](https://www.ernaehrungsindustrie.de/wie-die-glp-1-abnehmspritze-den-deutschen-lebensmittelmarkt-veraendert/) [B]). Juniper targets "particularly … women" ([Pulse 2.0, 6 Jun 2026](https://pulse2.com/hims-hers-completes-eucalyptus-acquisition-and-expands-weight-management-care-in-germany/) [B]).

## 4. Solution & product wedge
**Smallest product (wedge b, "Nach der Spritze"):** a 12-week digital programme for people who stop or taper:
1. A personal protein target and simple small-portion recipes.
2. Two 30-minute strength sessions a week, at home or in a gym, with progression.
3. A weekly check-in: weight, waist and strength.
4. An AI coach (chat) that answers food and training questions within set limits, with escalation to a human nutrition professional.
5. Optional, later: a private-label protein product that meets "source of protein" (≥12 % of energy from protein), so that the authorised muscle-mass claim can be used (section 8).

What AI/automation does: personalised plans and check-ins, coaching chat, automated nudges, and content generation reviewed by a nutrition professional. This is the CEO's skill set (`founder-brief.md`). *Inference.*

**What it is NOT (non-goals):**
- No dosing, dose-splitting or "click" calculators and no side-effect treatment advice. These would turn the app into medical-device software (MDCG 2019-11 rev.1, section 8).
- No drug sales, no prescriptions, no telemedicine.
- No "GLP-1" supplement and no claim to mimic the drug.
- No weight-loss promise.

**Wedge (a), during treatment, for comparison:** the same content sold to people on the drug. Not recommended: GoLighter, Juniper and Voy bundle coaching with the drug, and WW sells it for €11.25–45 (section 6).

- *Reading:* the TAM is small for a venture (€12.9m a year in Germany at one purchase per self-payer). It becomes venture-sized only if (1) the self-pay pool grows several-fold (tablet, price cuts, generics 2031), (2) the product reaches DACH/EU, (3) a repeat model is found, or (4) a payer route opens (section 7). None of these is in hand.

## 13. Validation plan
**The first draft's waitlist test is withdrawn** (red team F2): with no drug names in ads it would reach anyone who wants to keep weight off (WW's market), theme B ("low appetite") targeted people still on the drug (wedge a), and e-mails are not payments.

**Redesigned demand test: paid pre-order (for CEO approval later; nothing goes live without it):**
- **Do it only if** R3 is still open on the test date and the lawyer hour (section 8) finds a compliant wording.
- **Budget and duration:** €300 ad spend over 2–3 weeks, plus one paid lawyer hour (price not sourced) and a free landing-page tool.
- **One theme, aimed at the segment:** weight maintenance after a large weight loss, 12 weeks of strength training and protein. Channel options that reach leavers without naming the drug in the ad: (1) a sponsored post in an existing GLP-1 community, only with the admin's consent; (2) search ads on stop-related queries, if the platform allows such keywords (`[UNVERIFIED]`). Meta rules: 18+, no before/after, no negative body image (section 8).
- **Claims rules:** no drug names unless the lawyer clears them; no weight-loss promise; no promise to prevent regain; no disease claim.
- **What the visitor does:** pays **€129** for a place in a 12-week cohort that starts on a fixed date, with a **full refund** if the cohort does not start or on request before the start.
- **Pass mark (set in advance, my math):** **pass** = ≥ 10 paid pre-orders for €300 (≤ €30 each; leaves room for refunds below the €50–59 allowance); **kill** = < 6 paid pre-orders (> €50 each); 6–9 = inconclusive, default stop.
- **What a pass would and would not show:** that leavers pay at a cost per customer inside the allowance. It would **not** make this a seed-fundable company; that would still need a second market, a repeat model or a payer (red team, "What would change my mind").
- **What it needs (outward actions, CEO approval):** a legal seller with an Impressum, a payment account, terms with refund and withdrawal rules, and a GDPR-compliant form; if the CEO sells as a private person, a trade registration and possibly the employer's consent for a side business (*inference*; not checked).

**5–10 interviews (Mom Test, after the test or instead of it; outward action):**
- **Who:** 4 people who stopped a self-paid GLP-1 drug in the last 12 months after losing a lot of weight; 3 current self-payers; 1 pharmacist who dispenses many private prescriptions; 1 nutrition professional who coaches GLP-1 patients; 1 platform manager (GoLighter, Juniper or Voy).
- **Questions:**
  - "Why did you stop? What happened in the 3 months after?"
  - "What have you spent money on besides the drug (food, supplements, apps, coaching, gym)? How much a month?"
  - "What did you try to keep the weight off? What did it cost?"
  - "If the weight came back, would you go back on the drug, try a lower dose, or try something else?"
  - Platform: "What do you offer people who stop the drug? Would you pay per referred leaver?"
- **Confirms:** ≥ 3 of 7 patients **paid ≥ €50 for structured coaching or a programme aimed at keeping weight off** (a gym membership or a protein tub does not count), and ≥ 2 stoppers describe regain as their main worry.
- **Kills:** ≥ 4 of 7 would go back on the drug or a lower dose rather than buy a programme; or < 3 of 7 report such spend; or the platform already runs or plans a leaver programme.
