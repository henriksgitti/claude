# Consumer-health scan: FUTRUE-like opportunities (self-pay)
Task: C8 (parts 2–3) | Date: 2026-11-14 | Status: draft, complete (run 1: market frame, adapted scoring, 20 candidates; run 2: evidence filled, economics, funding fit, scoring, top 5, cheapest tests)

Product-type default used (CEO did not answer the open question in `progress.md`): **only products with published evidence count as mission-fit; homeopathy is excluded**; "evidence base" is a scored criterion.

## Findings
- **The self-pay market is large and grows without any payer contract.** Non-prescription medicines and health products in pharmacies (incl. mail order) sold €12.0bn in 2024 (+7.1 %); with drugstores and supermarkets IQVIA counts €13.8bn and 1.71bn packs; OTC mail order grew 10.7 % to €3.57bn ([IQVIA Q4 2024](https://iqvia.com/-/media/iqvia/pdfs/germany/library/publications/iqvia-pharma-marktbericht-classic-q4-2024_fin.pdf); [IQVIA/Healthcare Marketing 4/2025](https://www.iqvia.com/-/media/iqvia/pdfs/germany/publications/artikel-in-der-fachpresse/2025/artikel-otc-geschaeft-waechst-mai-2025.pdf) [B]).
- **Young brands can reach scale and exit, but marketing eats ~45 % of revenue and losses are common.** Marketing was 44 % of revenue at PharmaSGP (2019) and 46 % at Hims & Hers (2024: $678.8m of $1,476.5m) (filings [A]). Natsana (supplement brands, founded 2020, own shops + Amazon + dm/Rossmann) reached €87m revenue with a €19m net loss Bayer, which bought 30 % in 2022, announced a full takeover in July 2024 ([apotheke adhoc, Jul 2024](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/markt/bayer-uebernimmt-natsana-komplett/) [B]) and held 100 % from early 2025 ([Lebensmittel Praxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/industrie-aktuell/45294-trends-im-sortiment-kategorie-otc-bietet-chancen.html) [B]). Period-underwear brand Snuggs (founded 2019): €20m revenue, positive EBITDA in 2024 ([tech.eu, Feb 2025](https://tech.eu/2025/02/18/europe-s-most-funded-period-pants-snuggs-raises-5m/) [B]).
- **The ranking is flat (31–38 of 55 points) and every row is "unproven" on customer-acquisition cost**, which no desk source gives per category. Base top 5: **IBS (38), menopause (38), female bladder leaks (38), GLP-1 self-payer companion (37), fertility (36)**. Two themes stand out for a new brand: **midlife women (menopause + bladder leaks)** and **GLP-1 self-payers**; IBS and fertility are held by strong incumbents (Kijimea with an RCT; Femibion with 60 % share, 2016).
- **Where spend is proven, incumbents own the shelf; where the shelf is open, spend is unproven.** Under a strict K1 (€ or pack data for the category in DACH), only IBS, fertility, sleep, weight and lactose pass, all with a competition score of 1–2. Menopause, bladder leaks and GLP-1 companions pass only on use or adjacent spend (57.6 % of women with moderate-severe hot flushes used an OTC product [A]; "Wir sind auf dem Weg zu einem reinen Selbstzahler-Markt" for incontinence [B]; GLP-1 drug spend only [B]).
- **Claims law is the main regulatory constraint, not approval.** Supplements need only a notification to BVL (§ 5 NemV [A]), but disease claims ("hilft gegen …") are banned for all foods under Art. 7(3) LMIV, and most plant-substance claims are still unassessed by EFSA ([Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/projekt-klartext-nem/informationen/rechtliches/werbung-mit-gesundheit-meist-zu-viel-versprochen-13245) [B]). "GLP-1" supplements: "gibt es keine Wirknachweise … Die Werbung ist in der Regel unzulässig" ([Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/faq/glp1werbung-fuer-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-108473) [B]).
- **Grants hardly fit a product brand.** Start?Zuschuss! is for digital tech startups; EXIST needs a university host and no active job (`research/munich-ecosystem.md` [A]). *Inference:* only a digital part (coaching app, CBT programme) could qualify; the first product must be paid from the €10k and early sales.

### 2. Adapted scoring (applied in §6)
Knock-outs (from task C8):
- **K1 consumer spend proven**: a sourced figure shows consumers in DACH pay for products for this need today (sales, pack counts, a named brand's revenue, or a survey of use of paid products). Prevalence alone does not pass. **"Weak"** = use survey outside DACH, adjacent category, or spend on a different product (e.g. the drug, not the companion). Base ranking admits pass + weak; the strict ranking admits pass only (per the B4 lesson in `lessons.md`). **"Not shown"** = out.
- **K2 startable for ≤€50k incl. grants with a contract manufacturer**: route is a food supplement (BVL notification, § 5 NemV [A]), cosmetic, class I device, or a non-medical digital service. Class IIa+ devices, IVDs needing a notified body, drugs (incl. homeopathic registration) and prescription services fail K2 for the start product.

Criteria (1–5; weight): need size (×2), spend proof (×2), online reach and CAC (×2), competition gap (×1; 5 = open), evidence base for the product (×1), claims and regulatory risk (×1; 5 = low risk), margin (×1), founder fit (×1). Max 55. Confidence tags H/M/L and rules R1, R2 (an L score counts as at most 3) and R4 (sensitivity: every L −1 after the cap) as in `research/scoring.md`. **Change vs. `research/scoring.md`:** no "payer" or "procurement" criteria (the consumer pays); "online reach and CAC" replaces achievability, because the binding cost here is buying customers, not winning a contract. Criterion "online reach and CAC" is L for every row (no category CAC found), so every row is "unproven" in the sense of R3 and needs a cheap test.

**What this means for €10k (inference, illustrative numbers):**
- If a capsule product cost €3 per unit (not sourced), the 2,000-unit minimum costs €6,000, leaving ~€4,000 for ads, shop and samples. €10k therefore pays for **one product test, not a launch**.
- To reach €1m revenue at €30 a month: 1,000,000 / 360 = **~2,780 customers paying for a full year**. At the 44–46 % marketing share of the two filed companies, that needs **~€450k of marketing a year**, which must come from investors, media-for-equity or reinvested sales.
- First-order rule: at ~80 % gross margin and a €30 first order, a new customer pays back only if acquisition costs less than ~€24 (0.8 × 30) or the customer re-orders. Repeat rate, not first-order CAC, decides viability. No source gives repeat rates by category.

### 7. Top 5: sketches and cheapest tests
Tests marked **(CEO approval)** are outward actions (ads, sign-ups, contacts): not run.

1. **IBS: evidence-based gut supplement or service next to Kijimea.** Spend is proven (Kijimea ~$100m est. [B]), but the leader owns the RCT and the device route now needs a notified body (Rule 21, inference). A newcomer would sell a supplement with weaker claims against an RCT-backed leader. *Researcher view:* the worst place for a €10k entrant. Cheapest test (desk, 1 run): list the top 20 IBS products at two online pharmacies with price, claim type and review count; if no evidence-backed supplement gap shows, drop.
2. **Menopause: evidence-first midlife brand.** 9m women aged 40–55 [B]; OTC use is common [A]; funded startups (XbyX, Frieda, Evela, Femna) show investor appetite [B]. Desk test first (no approval needed): price and positioning table of the 4 DE startups and the top 10 pharmacy products.

Cheapest test: desk check whether DE telemedicine shops that sell the drug already bundle nutrition or coaching (if yes, partner or drop); then **(CEO approval)** €300 ad test to self-payers.

### 8. Researcher reading (inference)
- The FUTRUE pattern is proven, but the categories with proven spend are owned; a newcomer's chance is a **new or changing need** (menopause awareness, care shifting to self-pay for bladder leaks, GLP-1 self-payers), where spend is visible only indirectly.
- The binding unknown for all of them is **acquisition cost and repeat rate**, which only a small real-world test can measure. More desk research will not settle it.
- The menopause and bladder rows target the same women through the same channels and could be one brand family (FUTRUE builds brand families: Kijimea >20 products [B]).
