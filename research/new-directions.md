# New-directions scan: 10 business models beyond everything checked so far
Task: C15 (fixes C18) | Date: 2026-11-18 | Status: draft, revised after fact-check (C18, 2026-11-18; see `research/reviews/2026-11-18-new-directions.md`)

Method: 10 directions (business model + customer type). None of them re-ranks the long list, the shortlist, the consumer scan or any parked/dropped idea. Each gets the same quick test, built from the reasons earlier ideas failed (`decisions/`, latest red teams): **(K1)** someone already pays someone for this job today, **(K2)** a sourced price, **(K3)** how many customers three salaries (€360k a year) and €1m ARR need, **(K4)** free or cheap substitutes, **(K5)** founder edge (`founder-brief.md`), **(K6)** cheapest next test. Grades per `CLAUDE.md` rule 2. *My math* and *inference* are labelled. Prices marked `[UNVERIFIED]` are assumptions for the arithmetic only, not facts.

## Findings
- **Best fit: a document service for clinical-trial sponsors, run with AI and checked by people (direction 1). It is the only direction where a law creates the work, the work is late, and the founder's interests match.** Under EU rules, every sponsor must file a results summary *and* a lay summary in CTIS after a trial ends [A] ([EMA, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/other/joint-response-european-medicines-regulatory-network-letter-health-action-international-other-health-groups-missing-clinical-trial-results-ctis_en.pdf)). In the first study of compliance, only **116 of 234** trials that were due had reported on time [B, preprint] ([TranspariMED](https://www.transparimed.org/single-post/half-of-clinical-trials-of-medicines-in-europe-violate-new-transparency-law-new-study)). By the end of 2026, EMA will publish how many trials have result summaries [A]. The volume is about **2,232 new EU trials a year** (1,356 commercial + 876 non-commercial authorised in 2025; repo's own CTIS count [A], `research/trial-recruitment-de.md`). Each of these trials will need these documents when it ends. **Translations are recommended, not required by law:** the EU's own lay-summary guidance says "the EU CTR does not request translations", but recommends the summary "as a minimum" in the local language(s) of each trial country, matching the patient information sheet [A] ([Good Lay Summary Practice, EudraLex Vol. 10, 2021](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-10/glsp_en_0.pdf)). Paid AI vendors already exist: Peer AI raised $12.1m (2025) [B]; Yseop counts Novartis and Eli Lilly as investors and "150 clinical trials" [B]. **No per-summary price was found** `[UNVERIFIED]`. The only sourced rate is the freelance medical-writer median of **€82.2 an hour** (EMWA survey 2023, 223 respondents) [B] ([EMWA](https://emwa.org/journal/freelancing/seventh-freelance-business-survey-the-freelance-landscape-2018-2023/)).
- **Second: become the business co-founder of a research spin-out instead of building alone (direction 2).** The federal spin-out grant EXIST-Forschungstransfer funds teams of up to four full-time positions, **"davon mindestens eine Stelle mit betriebswirtschaftlichem Know-how"**. It also pays up to €250,000 for materials, for 18 months [A] ([BMWE one-pager, Nov 2025](https://exist.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/BMWE_Onepager_EXIST-Forschungstransfer_2025-DE_web-bf.pdf)). In 2025 there were 175 applications and >€55m approved. The average grant has been **>€1m per project since 2023**, and biotech, software and medtech lead [A] ([EFT monitoring report 2025](https://exist.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/EFT_Monitoringbericht_2025_Nr.3.pdf)). Only **634 of 2,280** applications since the start were approved ("Förderquote (gesamt) 27,8 %") [A] (same report). TUM researchers, students and alumni founded **115** start-ups in 2025 [B] ([TUM, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.tum.de/aktuelles/alle-meldungen/pressemitteilungen/details/presidential-award-fuer-raumfahrt-start-up)); most are student or alumni start-ups, not research spin-outs. *Inference:* this removes three of the old knock-outs at once. The grant pays salaries without customers, the science partner brings the edge the CEO lacks, and the CEO fills a seat that the rules require. But only about one application in four has been approved so far.
- **Third: a German-language admin team in Lisbon plus AI, sold to German healthcare buyers that already outsource per case (direction 3).** Buyers that already pay outside firms include a university hospital buying external coding revision (UK Leipzig, 2026; bidders need **≥€2m turnover**, references from ≥3 university or maximum-care hospitals, ISO 9001 and ≥2 employed coding physicians) [A] ([TED 342013-2026](https://api.ted.europa.eu/v3/notices/search)) and therapy and care providers paying billing centres (DMRZ: pre-financing "ab 1,30 %" plus €25 a month) [B] ([DMRZ](https://www.dmrz.de/fileadmin/Broschuere_Heilmittel.pdf)). Lisbon job ads for native German speakers pay **€1,634–1,906 gross a month** [C, expired 2025 ad] ([Blu Selection](https://www.bluselection.com/job/german-speaking-customer-service-in-lisbon-starting-2025)). The German minimum wage is €13.90 an hour in 2026 [A] ([Bundesregierung](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/mindestlohn-2026-2364720)). *Inference:* the edge (German + Lisbon + AI automation) is rare. The cost gap alone is modest (with the full quarterly bonus the Lisbon ad pays ~97 % of the German minimum wage; Details §3), and public buyers' turnover and reference bars block a new company.
- **Seven directions fail at least one old knock-out on desk evidence.** Pharmacovigilance outsourcing: the law forces the purchase (§ 63a AMG), but it needs a qualified-person co-founder, and EMA screens the literature for 400 substance groups free [A]. Buying a small health business: KfW says sellers ask a median of **0.6× annual revenue** [B], but the capital is out of reach. DiGA market entry: 74 DiGA admitted in total, only 58 left by end-2025 (use still rose 63 %) [A]; no price found. NIS2 compliance: ~1,000 extra health entities, but crowded and one-off [B]. Occupational health: mostly big incumbents win the tenders, plus a few local practices [A]. Insurer prevention courses: €243m a year, but capped at €75–150 a course and consumer-marketed [B]. Health data / RWE: the window is small or late (53 data applications to the new research data centre, 2 running AbD data collections; EHDS secondary use from 2029) [A/B].
- **Recommendation (inference):** run **direction 1** as the next deep dive, after two cheap desk tests: (1) count overdue CTIS results by sponsor type, Germany and Portugal; (2) find three published prices. Run **direction 2** as a parallel desk list of Munich spin-out projects that need a business co-founder. Keep direction 3 as a possible delivery model for direction 1, not as a venture of its own: multilingual lay summaries (DE/PT/EN) are EU-recommended good practice, not a legal duty, so the translation part only pays if sponsors follow the recommendation (to be checked in the price test). Details and options: task C16.

## Details

### 1. AI-assisted trial-results documents → trial sponsors (Top 1)
- **Who must pay [A]:** "Under Article 37 of the CTR, sponsors are legally required to submit a summary of trial results, accompanied by a layperson summary, to CTIS". The authorities send reminders and email alerts and track "timely submission … per Member State". The quarterly ACT EU reports "will include the number of trials with available summaries of results" by end-2026 ([EMA letter, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/other/joint-response-european-medicines-regulatory-network-letter-health-action-international-other-health-groups-missing-clinical-trial-results-ctis_en.pdf)).
- **The gap [B, preprint]:** "Of 234 drug trials that had become due to report results, only 116 had reported results on time". Another 98 had no results public, and 47 of the posted documents lacked data for all primary outcomes. CTIS holds ">8,400" relevant trials, most still running ([TranspariMED, May 2026](https://www.transparimed.org/single-post/half-of-clinical-trials-of-medicines-in-europe-violate-new-transparency-law-new-study); preprint not peer-reviewed). No split by sponsor type was given. An older EudraCT figure (snippet only, not opened): non-commercial 23.6 % vs commercial 77.2 % of results posted `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- **Volume [A, repo own count]:** 1,356 commercial + 876 non-commercial new EU trials authorised in 2025; Germany takes part in 668 commercial and 109 non-commercial (`research/trial-recruitment-de.md`). *My math:* 1,356 + 876 = 2,232 a year. Each ends with a results summary + lay summary.
- **Languages [A]:** the EU guidance says "While the EU CTR does not request translations", the expert group recommends that "as a minimum, the LS should be provided in the local language(s) of each of the countries where the trial took place, matching the languages employed in the PIS/ICF", plus English "where resources allow". It also says "While one version must be uploaded to the EU Portal within the legally required timeline, no time frame for additional translated LS is defined", and suggests sponsors "may consider engaging with a language service provider" ([Good Lay Summary Practice, EudraLex Vol. 10, adopted 9 Jul 2021, pp. 48–49](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-10/glsp_en_0.pdf); same rule in the 2017 Commission expert-group recommendations, §9, read via a [mirror](https://gmp-compliance.org/files/guidemgr/2017_01_26_summaries_of_ct_results_for_laypersons.pdf)). So: one lay summary is a legal duty; translations are recommended good practice, not law. No national German rule requiring a German results lay summary was found (2 searches) `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- **Funding gap for academic sponsors [A]:** the same guidance warns that "most funding bodies do not foresee budget allocation for LS preparation", and that because the lay summary is often due after the funding period, "it may be impossible to secure funds for LS" (GLSP Handbook §2.3, p. 22). *Inference:* academic sponsors have the duty but often no budget; this weakens first idea (c) below.
- **Who sells today [B]:** Peer AI (San Francisco; $12.1m, 2025) drafts regulatory documents such as protocols and clinical study reports; its customers include "top 20 pharmaceutical firms and emerging biotechs" ([eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/peer-ai-raises-12-1m-to-speed-up-drug-approvals-with-agentic-ai)); lay summaries are not named in the article. Yseop (France) got investment from "Novartis (dRx Capital)" with "Eli Lilly" and claims "more than 150 clinical trials" and "10,000+ reports" in 2023 (vendor claim, ~3 years old) ([Yseop, 5 Dec 2023](https://yseop.com/news-and-press-releases/yseop-announces-strategic-investment-and-celebrates-milestone-of-150-clinical-trials-powered-by-generative-ai/)). TrialAssure (est. 2009) generated PLS for "nearly 50,000 documents" with its LINK GenAI platform (vendor claim, ~3 years old) ([PRWeb, 18 Oct 2023](https://www.prweb.com/releases/trialassure-uses-artificial-intelligence-to-create-plain-language-summaries-for-50-000-studies-listed-on-clinicaltrialsgov-301960306.html)). No named small-biotech or academic customer was found.
- **Price:** none published (5 searches; KKS fee schedules, EMWA, vendor pages; the Citeline page with "$3,000 to $3,500" for a patient panel review returned 404, snippet only `[UNVERIFIED]`). The only sourced input is the freelance median of €82.2/h (2023, 223 respondents) [B] ([EMWA](https://emwa.org/journal/freelancing/seventh-freelance-business-survey-the-freelance-landscape-2018-2023/)). The data is 3 years old.
- **K3 (my math, assumed prices):** at €5,000 a trial package, €360k = 72 packages and €1m = 200. At €10,000: 36 and 100. 100–200 packages = 4.5–9.0 % of one year's EU trials (100 / 2,232; 200 / 2,232). That is many fewer customers than L01 (167–279) or GLP-1 (~7,750 a year), because one sponsor brings several trials.
- **K4 substitutes:** sponsors' own writers with general AI tools; CROs bundling the job; the vendors above. *Inference:* the opening is the sponsors that are late and small (academic sponsors and small biotechs), plus languages. That is also where willingness to pay is least proven.
- **K5 edge:** drug development is the CEO's stated interest; AI agent delivery is his job. German, English and Portuguese are three EU trial languages, but translations are only recommended (see Languages), so this edge counts only for sponsors that follow the recommendation. Gap: a medical/regulatory writer co-founder (EMWA network).
- **Concrete first ideas:** (a) "CTR results pack": results summary + lay summary + translations for sponsors with overdue CTIS results, fixed price per trial; (b) *conditional on sponsors following the EU translation recommendation:* lay-summary translation and readability testing in DE/PT/ES for CROs (sub-contracting, no tender); (c) the same pack for academic sponsors through the university trial centres (KKS/ZKS); weak, because the EU guidance says most funders do not budget for lay summaries (see above).
- **K6 cheapest tests:** desk: pull the CTIS public API (works, repo recipe) and count trials ended >12 months ago with no results, by sponsor type and country (DE, PT), and name the sponsors; find 3 published prices (CRO or LSP price lists, tender awards in other EU countries). Real-world (needs approval): 5 calls with clinical-operations leads at small biotechs or KKS.

### 3. German-language admin team in Lisbon + AI → German healthcare buyers that already outsource (Top 3, as delivery model)
- **Who pays today [A/B]:** UK Leipzig tenders "Externe Kodierrevision"; it has ~35 own coders and >60,000 inpatient cases. The bars: "Mindestens 2 Mio. EUR brutto Umsatz pro abgeschlossenes Geschäftsjahr", plus "Referenzen von mindestens drei Akutkrankenhäusern der Maximalversorgung oder Universitätskliniken" over ≥24 months, ISO 9001, and "mindestens zwei fest angestellten Ärzten" with ≥5 years of coding experience (field `selection-criterion-description-lot`) [A] ([TED 342013-2026](https://api.ted.europa.eu/v3/notices/search)). Kassen outsource invoice scanning (AOK Hessen; repo `research/pain-industry.md`) [A]. Billing centres: DMRZ "Vorfinanzierung – bereits ab 1,30 %", plus €25 a month for therapy providers [B] ([DMRZ](https://www.dmrz.de/fileadmin/Broschuere_Heilmittel.pdf)).
- **Labour [A/B/C]:** Lisbon, native German: €1,633.89–1,905.92 gross a month plus a quarterly bonus (expired 2025 ad) [C] ([Blu Selection](https://www.bluselection.com/job/german-speaking-customer-service-in-lisbon-starting-2025)). Portugal's minimum wage is €920 a month in 2026 (Decreto-Lei 139/2025; secondary sources) [B] ([APCMC](https://apcmc.pt/legislacao/salario-minimo-nacional-para-2026-fixado-em-e-920/)). Germany: €13.90 an hour [A]. The ad adds "bonus up to 1.267,00€ gross per quarter"; the lower option includes free accommodation. *My math:* €13.90 × 40 h × 52 / 12 ≈ €2,409 a month at the German minimum. Base pay is 68–79 % of that (1,633.89 / 2,409; 1,905.92 / 2,409). With the full bonus (1,267 / 3 ≈ €422 a month), the no-accommodation option is ≈ €2,328 = ~97 % (2,328 / 2,409). The real gap is larger for skilled roles (coders), but no DE coder wage was sourced.
- **K4:** incumbents (Tiplu/Kodierbörse, SPS, DMRZ, opta data). Public tenders demand turnover a new firm lacks. Health data abroad: the § 393 SGB V cloud rules apply (repo `research/parked-ideas-screen.md`).
- **Reading (inference):** strong edge, weak standalone case. It works best as the production team for direction 1 (multilingual documents), where no tender bar applies.

### 4. Pharmacovigilance service for small German MAHs (park)
- Legal duty [A]: every MAH must appoint a "Stufenplanbeauftragter" in the EU, and "andere Personen … dürfen eine Tätigkeit als Stufenplanbeauftragter nicht ausüben" ([§ 63a AMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/amg_1976/__63a.html)).
- Customers [B]: ">650 pharma companies" in Germany ([GTAI](https://www.gtai.de/gtai-en/invest/industries/life-sciences/pharmaceuticals)); "Rund 84 Prozent … kleine und mittlere Betriebe" ([Apotheke Adhoc on BPI OTC data](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/branchennews/alle-branchennews/branchennews-detail/otc-markt-waechst-und-entlastet-das-gesundheitssystem/)). *My math:* 84 % × ~670 ≈ 563 SMEs (670 is a search snippet; GTAI's ">650" was opened).
- Free substitute [A]: EMA monitors the literature for "400 active substance groups" (since 2015; data >3 years old, still the published scope) ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/safety-monitoring-medicines-ema-screen-medical-literature-400-active-substance-groups)). Paid services exist: e.g. DDReg sells "Retainer services for QPPV Services" and PV outsourcing in Germany [B] ([DDReg](https://www.ddregpharma.com/pharmacovigilance-services-in-germany)). Other named incumbents (PharmaLex, Freyr, ProductLife, AI tool biologit) not opened `[UNVERIFIED]`. No price published (3 searches) `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- Why park: the core role needs a qualified person and carries personal liability. That is a co-founder the CEO cannot replace.
