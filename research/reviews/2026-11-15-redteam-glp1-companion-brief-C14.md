## Fatal or near-fatal objections
None new for the decision. The venture-level objections of the first review (F1, F3, F4) stand and are now stated in the brief itself. The following caveats concern option (b) only.

**C1. The test's own precondition is already failed; the brief re-reads its kill line to keep the option open.**
- Section 12, R3 kill line: "**Any one** of GoLighter, Juniper, Voy or WW offers DE leavers a maintenance path free or at ≤ €15/month." Status written beside it: "met in the loose reading (WW's generic €11.25 plan is open to leavers); not met in the strict reading (no DE leaver-specific track found)".
- Section 13: "**Do it only if** R3 is still open on the test date."
- The kill line as written does not require a *leaver-specific* track; it requires "a maintenance path". WW's plan is a maintenance path at €11.25 open to anyone, and WW's own blog tells leavers that members who "stayed engaged … after discontinuing their medication continued to see results" (section 6). The first red team set this line precisely because "WW alone is open to anyone" (P2). Adding a "strict reading" after the fact is moving the goal post.
- **Buyer:** "Why would I pay €129 for 12 weeks when WW, which I may already know from GoLighter, costs €11.25 a month and has protein targets and strength videos?" The brief has no answer beyond "German-language, leaver-specific framing", which nachderspritze.de and formoline already occupy.
- Effect: on the brief's own rules, option (b) is closed today. The CEO should read R3 as **met**.

**C2. The test does not measure scalable acquisition cost, which is the stated reason to run it.**
- Bottom line: "If you still want to learn what a paying consumer costs (useful for the midlife-women theme too), the only test worth running is the redesigned pre-order test." Task C12 notes: "Choose (b) only to learn consumer acquisition cost for the consumer route in general."
- Section 13 channels: (1) "a sponsored post in an existing GLP-1 community, only with the admin's consent"; (2) "search ads on stop-related queries, if the platform allows such keywords (`[UNVERIFIED]`)".
- Channel (1): glp-1forum.de has "1.200+ Mitglieder" (section 2). A post's price is whatever the admin asks, possibly zero, so "€300 of ads" is not defined. A warm, small, self-selected forum is the most favourable audience there is; it does not repeat at scale, and it says nothing about cost per customer for menopause or bladder-leak buyers (*inference*). The forum founder runs a free site on the same topic and could also be a competitor or partner, which biases the admin's consent and placement.
- Channel (2) is unverified, and if keywords like "Abnehmspritze absetzen" are not allowed, nothing is left except generic maintenance ads, which F2 already rejected.
- *My math* on noise: at the pass line of 10 pre-orders and the kill line of 6, the counts are so small that one forum post going well or badly decides the result. The inconclusive band 6–9 with "default stop" is right; it means most outcomes end in stop.
- **Investor:** "A forum post that sold ten seats is a nice anecdote. It is not a CAC, and it is not a CAC for a different product."

**C3. The true cost and obligation of option (b) are understated.**
- Section 13 budget: "€300 ad spend … plus one paid lawyer hour (price not sourced) and a free landing-page tool." Preconditions listed: Impressum, payment account, terms, GDPR form, possibly trade registration and employer consent.
- Missing: **delivery.** A pass means ≥10 people paid €129 for "a place in a 12-week cohort that starts on a fixed date". Section 4 and section 8 ("Safety design") require "escalation to a human nutrition professional" and "a named human professional" for people who just stopped an obesity drug, "many with diabetes or other conditions". Section 11 has no named nutrition co-founder; R8's date is 31 Mar 2027. So a pass forces either (i) hiring a professional and running a 12-week programme while working full-time in Lisbon, or (ii) refunding everyone, which burns trust in the forum channel.
- Not listed as a precondition: **a named nutrition professional who will deliver the cohort.** It should be, on the brief's own safety design.
- *My math:* €1,290 of revenue from a pass (10 × €129) against COGS of ~€26 per person (section 5) leaves no money for a professional's time beyond minutes per participant, which the brief itself flags (section 5, COGS).

**C4. The product-evidence sentence still overstates (carry-over from fact-check C14, issue 1).**
- Section 1: "people who had done supervised exercise *during* a year of liraglutide regained 6.0 kg less in the year after stopping than those on the drug alone". Correct: 6.0 kg was exercise alone vs drug alone; exercise plus drug vs drug alone was 2.5 kg, not significant.
- Reasoning effect: the brief uses this as "the closest evidence" that the product helps. The correct reading says the trial's clear benefit belongs to people who exercised instead of taking the drug, which is the WW / generic diet market, not wedge (b). Together with SURMOUNT-MAINTAIN (lower dose kept most weight off), the best-evidenced options for a leaver are "a lower dose" and "general lifestyle", neither of which this product owns.

**C5. The upstream ranking still carries the old score.**
- `research/consumer-health-scan.md` line 116 keeps "GLP-1 self-payer companion … **37**", rank 4, and line 139 "Best founder fit (coaching and automation)". The brief header says the critic's re-score is 31/55 and "I did not re-score formally".
- Effect: any later pick from the consumer scan (e.g. after C12 (d), choosing the next consumer theme) starts from a top 5 with one row that, on today's evidence, does not belong there. The fix belongs to the scan, not the brief; it is in scope of the open C10 red-team half, so it is listed here and not added as a task.

**C6. Interview confirm line is easier than it looks.**
- Section 13: "Confirms: ≥ 3 of 7 patients paid ≥ €50 for structured coaching or a programme aimed at keeping weight off". The 7 are 4 stoppers + 3 current self-payers. Current self-payers on GoLighter/Juniper/Voy get coaching inside the drug price; those with a physician route may have paid for WW. Counting them mixes wedge (a) spend into the wedge (b) test.
- Sharper: count only the 4 stoppers; confirm if ≥ 2 of 4 paid ≥ €50 for a keep-weight-off programme **after** stopping (*my threshold*). Recruiting 4 recent stoppers who lost a lot is itself the reach problem of C2; if the CEO cannot find 4 in two weeks through his own network, that is a data point.
