# Review: research/trends.md (A1)
Date: 2026-11-06 | Verdict: pass (minor fixes)

Strong file. 21 of ~40 sources were re-opened via curl on 2026-11-06. Nearly all numbers match their source exactly. The issues below are about framing and one missing precedent that matters for the file's main inference (PNOG fund → B2B route).

## Critical issues (must fix)
None blocking. Should-fix items (task A10):
1. **Rating Report 8.5% is a conditional, presented as a forecast** (Findings bullet 4; §5; inference table row 3 "8.5% non-nursing FTE cut"). Source: "Um das … Sparpaket abzufangen, **müssten** die Kliniken bis 2030 rund 8,5 Prozent an Vollzeitstellen außerhalb der Pflege abbauen" ([AOK G+G](https://www.aok.de/pp/gg/update/krankenhaeuser-insolvenzen-rating/)). This is the cut needed to absorb the savings law, not a projected cut. The inference table builds a startup opening on it. Reword it as "would need to cut".
2. **Draft laws stated as settled.** Findings bullet 5: "e-referrals **become** mandatory from 1 Sep 2029". GeDIG is at first reading (§6 says "in progress"), and the AOK source says "**sollen** … verpflichtet werden". Same for PNOG amounts: the bullet says "Draft", but the inference table states "PNOG €1.6bn digital fund (from Jul 2027)" without the caveat. Add "(draft; may change)" wherever these feed an inference.
3. **Missing precedent for the PNOG inference: the existing §8 Abs. 8 SGB XI digital subsidy.** The PNOG draft itself ([RefE p. 24](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/3_Downloads/Gesetze_und_Verordnungen/GuV/P/RefE-Pflegeneuordnungsgesetz_PNOG.pdf)) shows that ambulatory/day-care facilities have had a one-off grant since 2019 (up to 40% of spend, max **€12,000 per facility**, extended to 2030; also in force law: [gesetze-im-internet §8 SGB XI](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_11/__8.html)). How much of it was drawn down is the best available evidence on whether new money will actually turn into purchases. If uptake was low, "€1.6bn fund = budget for a startup" is weak. Also missing from the inference: the €1.6bn goes to **facilities via Pflegekassen**. The per-facility amount, conditions (incl. "Nachweis der Wirtschaftlichkeit") and procedure are only set in GKV-SV guidelines **due 30 Jun 2027** (RefE §8 Abs. 6). So the earliest realistic revenue from this route is H2 2027. Route this to A9.
4. **Pflegestatistik misreadings (minor).** §2 says the residential-care "absolute number [is] not in the release". It is: "14 % beziehungsweise 800 000" ([Presseportal](https://www.presseportal.de/pm/32102/5933248)). Also "only ~100,000 of that is demographic, the rest is due to the broader 2017 definition": Destatis says the rise "geht **zum Teil** auf den weiter gefassten Pflegebegriff zurück". It does not attribute all of the remainder to the definition. And the 4.9m "at home" includes 680,000 Pflegegrad-1 people, 640,000 of whom used no services. This matters for any caregiver/home-care TAM.

## Missing evidence
- **Uptake of past provider-digitisation money**: §8 Abs. 8 SGB XI (care, since 2019) and KHZG (hospitals). Without this, "public money is flowing to providers" is a supply-side statement, not demand.
- **Who already sells into the PNOG-eligible categories** (care documentation software: incumbents such as Vivendi/Connext, MediFox, DAN, Snap; names `[UNVERIFIED]`, for A4/A5 to check). If the fund mostly buys upgrades from incumbents, a startup wedge is narrow.
- **Number of PNOG-eligible facilities** (ambulante Dienste + Tagespflege), needed to estimate the average grant per facility.
- **Manufacturer view on DiGA** (SVDGV): the author already flags it.
- EU long-term-care/cost data: flagged by the author. Only matters once EU expansion is scored.

## Assumptions to challenge
- "Payers are cutting costs, new public digitisation money flows to providers → B2B to providers is safer than DiGA." Plausible, but both halves need testing: (a) providers under price caps (Grundlohnrate −1pp) and deficits may cut software spend too, and the money in a grant fund is not money spent; (b) care services have notoriously thin IT capacity, and only 50% have even activated their SMC-B a year after the mandate. That is evidence of **low adoption capacity**, which cuts both ways: a pain for them, but also slow sales for a startup.
- "Every trend below follows from [demographics]": a rhetorical overreach. The Pflege growth is largely definitional (issue 4), and the hospital deficits are policy-driven. This matters because definitional growth can be reversed by policy: PNOG tightens Pflegegrad 1–3 assessment (Berliner Zeitung, cited in §2).
