# Red team: ventures/eldercare-navigator/brief.md (C1, L01)
Date: 2026-11-13 | Verdict: **needs work** (the brief) / **fail as a venture case** (wedge (ii), employer-paid eldercare alone)

Mode RED TEAM. Facts are taken as checked in `2026-11-12-eldercare-navigator-brief.md`; this review attacks the reasoning only. No new sources were opened; every number below is from the brief or the fact-check, and every calculation is marked *my math*. Points already made in `2026-11-08-redteam-shortlist.md` (R1 bundles, R2 founder fit, R4 no L03 co-payer) are not repeated; the brief answered them honestly.

**Summary for the CEO.**
- The brief is honest and its direction is right: do not go to Stage D as written. The "no paying-customer proof" finding is **fair, and understated** (F1).
- Its recommended option (b) is under-argued. The 5–8 HR conversations test an assumption that is **not the binding one**. Even if every HR lead says yes, the brief's own best case (60 customers, €359,000 ARR) does not pay the three people the brief says are needed. That is answerable on the desk today (F2).
- The kill criteria and the pilot design contradict the brief's own usage estimate by a factor of about 7; as written the pilot is failed before it starts (F3).
- The re-wedge towards Pflegebegleitung ("€760m a year") is a budget, not a market, and it leads to the buyer the scoring already rated closed to a new company (F5).
- **My recommendation for C3 (you decide): (c) for the employer wedge, with one cheap desk run kept from (b)**: read the PNOG as passed after 11 Dec 2026 and check whether any Kasse buys third-party tools or delivery. No HR conversations unless the desk finds a price point ≥€15,000 per employer per year (*my threshold*).

## Fatal or near-fatal objections

**F1. Fatal for wedge (ii): the unit of purchase is a bundle, and the brief's own evidence shows weak willingness to pay, not an open market.**
- Section 2 leaves it open: "That is either an open market or weak willingness to pay; the survey cannot tell which." The rest of the brief can tell. All of it points one way:
  - Offer rate 14.0 % (2015) → 15.7 % (2026) while pme (">1400 Unternehmen"), famPlus, awo, voiio ("1000+") and heycare ("Über 450") sold the whole time. Eleven years of selling moved the number 1.7 points.
  - The one eldercare-only vendor with a public price (Eldercare24, €299–499 a month) shows no customer and is a side product of a training company. That is what "eldercare alone for the Mittelstand" looks like after someone tried it.
  - Free employer-side substitutes exist and are used: NRW state programme with 690 employers, Familienpakt Bayern Pflegelotsen trainings. Only 2.4 % of firms even take the free route.
  - The only young entrant with named customers, heycare (2022), won with a bundle (childcare first, 35,000 carers) and €4m. That proves a new company can enter this market, and it proves **how**: not with eldercare alone.
- The desk test "company ≤3 years old, eldercare only, named paying employer" was narrow (4 searches, 6 sites; small B2B vendors rarely publish customers), so "none found" alone would be weak. Together with the four items above it is strong. **The finding is fair.**
- "Focus on firms the bundlers do not court" (section 4) has no evidence. heycare's 450 and voiio's 1,000+ customers cannot all be large firms (*inference*); section 12 itself notes that famPlus and pme train Pflegelotsen in Bavaria. The Familienpakt channel in section 9 is the incumbents' channel.
- **Buyer (managing director, 300 staff, no family-service contract):** "I have no budget line for this. That is why I have no contract. Two or three of my people a year would use it (your number). The Pflegestützpunkt is free. You are two people with no reference, and you want 24 months."

**F2. Fatal for the venture case: the numbers do not carry a company even if every assumption holds.**
- Brief, section 5: SOM "30–60 customers within 24 months ... €108,000–359,000 ARR. This does not carry two founders' salaries." Section 11 asks for **three** people (CEO, care-counselling co-founder, product engineer).
- Size does not rescue the price. After the C4 fix the per-head benchmark is €4–6 per employee per year (one buyer's estimate, no award). *My math:* a 1,000-staff firm pays €4,000–6,000, the same as a 100-staff firm on the list price. There is no price lever in the evidence.
- *My math:* €1m ARR needs 167–279 customers at €3,588–5,988. Contract value over 24 months is €7,176–11,976; the brief says this "does not pay for field sales". The 30–60 figure has no funnel behind it: no channel in section 9 is verified as open, and the one comparable (Eldercare24, public price, website) shows zero customers.
- The kill row "No route to >€1m ARR within 3 years of full-time start" cannot fail, because a "route" can always be drawn. Section 5 already states the condition (">500 customers, a per-head price at larger firms, or a second payer") and the brief finds evidence for none of the three. On its own criterion the idea is at the kill line **today**.
- **Investor (Munich seed):** "Services revenue, €4–6k contracts, one adviser as the product, four funded or decades-old incumbents in the same city, and the state ships a free version in 2028–29. Where is the 10x? Wellthy is not a comparable: US employers carry health costs and there is no statutory care insurer; $450 a month per enrolled employee versus €299 a month per **company** is a gap of two orders of magnitude and argues against the transfer, not for it."
- Consequence for option (b): the HR conversations can confirm F1 or soften it. They cannot repair F2. A founder with a few part-time hours a week should not spend them on a test whose best outcome leaves the idea unfundable.

**F3. Near-fatal for the validation design: the pilot and two kill rows contradict the brief's own usage estimate.**
- Section 7: "at 8 % carers and perhaps one in ten of them opening a case per year, a 300-person firm produces 2–3 cases a year". *My math:* 0.8 cases per 100 staff per year, 0.4 per 100 in six months, i.e. 1–2 cases per pilot firm.
- Section 9 converts a pilot "if ≥3 % of staff open a case" (in 6 months): 9 cases at 300 staff. That is about **7 times** the brief's own expectation.
- Section 12 kills at "<1 case per 100 staff in 6 months": 2.5 times the expectation. The pilot fails by design, or the usage estimate is wrong; the brief must pick one.
- Three firms × 6 months gives about 4 cases in total. "HR rates it ≥8/10" then rests on one or two cases per firm. No pilot of this size can separate 0.4 from 1.0 per 100.
- With 1–2 cases a firm, any usage report identifies the employee. The brief needs usage to convert and renew, and promises HR "No usage report by person"; at n < 5 an aggregate is a report by person. Renewal without usage proof is the churn mechanism heycare names ("Geringe Nutzung – oft unter 3%").
- A pilot at "€0–1,500" with a "signed intent" does not test payment. Section 13 asks for "past behaviour, not opinions"; the first kill row ("0 of 8 HR leads would pay ≥€250 a month") is an opinion, and its threshold lets one polite yes keep the idea alive.
- "≥6 of 8 already covered" cannot be measured: section 13 recruits "3 without a family-service contract, 2 with" on purpose. Coverage has to be counted at screening, before selection.
- "A bundler offers a care-only tier at ≤€300 a month" may already be met: Eldercare24 is at €299, and famPlus lists Pflegeberatung as its own offering. Not checked by the brief.

**F4. Near-fatal: nothing in the plan uses this founder, and the plan needs more than the founder has.**
- `founder-brief.md`: no clinical, scientific or engineering background; strength is AI agent implementation; €10,000; part-time in Lisbon until September 2027.
- The brief's "honest limit" says AI is not a difference ("every element above already exists"). So the founder's one edge is not the product's edge. What remains for the CEO is "sales and operations": relationship sales to Munich HR, from Lisbon, part-time, with no known HR contact (the founder-access check from 8 Nov is still unanswered).
- The product **is** the care adviser. A qualified Pflegeberater:in is asked to co-found a company whose best-case revenue cannot pay them. The brief gives no reason why that person would not join famPlus or pme, or, from 2029, a Kasse hiring Pflegebegleitpersonen with a funded budget.
- Money: "Capital to first revenue €8,000–15,000 ... at or above the €10,000 own budget". EXIST needs "no active job" and an innovation test the brief doubts; venture money is "unlikely". The funding path in section 10 is therefore one grant of max €36,000 after founding. `founder-brief.md` requires an idea that reaches "a grant, pre-seed or paying pilot before needing more".
- Timing: the brief's "why now" is the rule change on 1 Jan 2027. The founder is full-time nine months later. Launch (3–4 months) plus a 6-month pilot puts the first converted contract in mid-2028, the month the Pflege-Cockpit is due, on a 24-month term that runs into 2030.

**F5. Near-fatal for the re-wedge in option (b): "€760m a year" is a statutory delivery budget, not an addressable market.**
- Section 8 and option (b) present Pflegebegleitung as "a possible new buyer", "€760m a year from 2029". That figure is €146 per care recipient for home visits and case management by qualified staff. The brief shows no evidence that any part of it buys third-party software, and none that a Kasse would hand delivery to a company founded in 2027.
- Who gets a delegated task: bodies with staff and history. The bill's own reasoning says some Kassen have "already" handed counselling to third parties, so those third parties exist and hold the references. Municipalities may take the task over by 31 Dec 2027. Nui Care already sits at AOK Bayern and DAK.
- The buyer is signer cluster C5 (Pflegekasse). `research/scoring.md` already treats that route as tender-with-references plus small direct awards; L03 was scored "unproven" on the same buyer. The re-wedge moves from a buyer who pays little to a buyer a new company cannot reach. Calling it "a different company" (section 8) is correct; offering it as the hopeful half of the recommended option is not.
- It is a bill with a €7.6bn financing gap and announced amendments. Building a venture on an unfunded mandate that starts 16 months after the founding date is a bet on the legislator.

**F6. PNOG weighting (the question in task C2): right direction, wrong mechanism.**
- **As a substitute for use: slightly over-weighted.** A free statutory entitlement (§ 7a counselling) has existed for years, only "3 Prozent" use it, and employers still buy bundles. Free and statutory has not removed demand so far. Kassen IT deadlines slip. What is new, and the brief should say so, is that § 7c is proactive ("einen konkreten Termin") and yearly for Pflegegeld recipients; that is what could change uptake.
- **As a sales objection: under-weighted.** The damage arrives before 2029. An HR lead asked in 2027–28 to sign 24 months will hear "the Kasse does this free from 2029". The bill kills the purchase argument before it kills the need. Section 12's kill row misses this.
- That kill row ("§§ 7a, 7c, 7d pass unchanged **and** the pilot value lies mostly after the first care grade") joins a December 2026 event to a 2028 pilot result, so it cannot fire before the money is spent. And the product in section 4 (48-hour checklist, care-grade application, MD report) sits **before** the first care grade, where § 7c does not reach. The honest residual product is a one-off episode of a few weeks per employee. That is small, and it is the part Nui gives away through insurers.
- **As a new market: over-weighted** (F5).

**F7. Method: the brief was not re-scored, and by the project's own knock-outs wedge (ii) does not pass.**
- Header: "Not re-scored here; the new findings ... would lower it further." L01 still stands at rank 1 (64/60) in `research/shortlist.md`, and option (c) compares it with parked ideas on stale scores.
- K1 (`research/scoring.md`): "the wedge's signer type pays today for the same job"; "A price list counts only with a named paying customer". For wedge (ii) the signer is a private mid-size employer buying eldercare alone. Evidence: one price list without a customer, and lots at public employers (a different signer type, bought by tender). Strict reading: **K1 not shown.**
- K2: "a company ≤5 years old with a named paying customer" for this signer type. The brief's own desk test: "None found". heycare counts for a bundle, not for this wedge. **K2 unproven at best.**
- The additive score let this happen: pain 4, market 4, achievability 4, founder fit 5 outvoted competition 1–2 and unproven willingness to pay. The B4 note already said that under a strict K1 "L01 drops out". The brief's evidence is the strict reading.
- The problem evidence does not reach the payer. Lost hours (184,000–264,000 FTE) are a cost to the economy and to the carer. Nothing in section 2 shows that navigation help reduces absence or hours cut; "625,000 cut their working hours" follows from ~3 hours of care per working day, not from paperwork. The one-liner's "the employer loses fewer hours" has no evidence item. User ≠ payer, and the payer's return is not shown.

## Fixes for the one fix round (fold into task C4; edits only, no new research)
1. **Section 12, kill column:** replace with dated, measurable criteria (K-a to K-h above, or the researcher's own numbers); remove "No route to >€1m ARR"; decouple the PNOG row from the pilot.
2. **Sections 7, 9, 12:** make the usage numbers agree (0.8 cases per 100 staff per year vs "≥3 % of staff" vs "<1 per 100 in 6 months"); say what a 3-firm pilot can and cannot show.
3. **Section 13:** count contract coverage at screening, not in a sample chosen by coverage; make the confirm bar a signed paid pilot.
4. **Sections 8 and 12 (option b):** state that €760m is the Kassen's delivery budget, that no third-party software or new-company share is shown, and that the buyer is cluster C5 with the K2 problems already recorded for L03.
5. **Section 5 or header:** add the customer-count arithmetic (three salaries; €1m ARR) and the K1/K2 reading for wedge (ii) under `research/scoring.md`. A full re-score is not needed.
6. **Section 2:** resolve "open market or weak willingness to pay" with the brief's own evidence, or say why it stays open; mark "the employer loses fewer hours" as unproven.
7. **Section 6:** label Wellthy as a non-comparable (US payer system, price per enrolled employee).

## Caveats that stay after the fix round (rule 14; no further tasks)
- Vendor customer counts are claims; the coverage of mid-size firms by bundlers is unknown and only outreach can measure it.
- All PNOG reasoning depends on the text as passed.
- The usage rate (one carer in ten opens a case) is the researcher's guess; no German source for employer-paid eldercare usage is in the brief.
- No survivorship check was run: failed eldercare-only or carer-navigation startups in Germany were not searched for. Their absence from the brief is not evidence that none tried.
- My thresholds (€15,000, 0.5 cases per 100, 9 of 15, 2 of 8) are judgment calls, not sourced.
