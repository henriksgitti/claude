### Caveats (rule 14: listed for the CEO, no new tasks)
**K1. The reachable market prices 1–2-country trials as if they bought four languages.** §5 values the 181 reachable trials at "€3,500–6,000" and §7 defines €3,500 as "EN + up to 4 languages". *My math* from the brief's own tables: core trials by country count are 107 (1 country, Phase II–IV), 89 (2 countries), 231 (3+). 3+-country core trials without a large CRO = 231 − 178 = **53**, so **128 of 181 (71 %)** of the "reachable" trials run in one or two countries. Their pack is EN + 1–2 languages; direct cost about €0.75–1.8k (writer €335–838 + €418–461 per language, §7). The buyer compares that to a freelance writer's day (§13: ~€650–800) and R5 already kills at <€1,500 for EN + 1 language. At €1,500–2,500 for those 128 and €3,500–6,000 for the 53: 128 × 1,500–2,500 = €192–320k; 53 × 3,500–6,000 = €186–318k; **total ≈ €0.38–0.64m** a year for sponsors from anywhere, before US-based sponsors are removed as unreachable from Munich. The €360k-contribution line then needs well over 100 % of the reachable pool at either price. Direction: strengthens (a).

**K2. §13 step 1 cannot change any decision.** "Pass: ≤2 missing items per summary. A failure here kills the AI-drafting step, not the idea." Annex V is a fixed 10-heading template; any current LLM given the headings will produce text under each, so a pass is near-certain and says nothing about accuracy, tone or writer time (the brief itself says it "cannot test factual accuracy or writer time"). A fail does not stop the idea either. A test whose result cannot move the decision is busywork for a part-time CEO (`CLAUDE.md`: "no busywork"). If (b) is chosen, skip step 1 and start with step 2.

**K3. The "confirm" line of T3 is also the founder-fit kill.** §13 step 2: confirms if "the writer says small sponsors buy lay summaries outside the CRO contract and names a price ≥€2,500". *Inference:* that answer proves a market **for freelance writers**, i.e. that the writer can sell alone (F2, R4). The conversation should carry a second question that tests R4 directly: "Would you split a business with a non-writer whose share is earned by sales and AI set-up?" Without it, a "pass" on T3 moves the CEO toward a venture in which he is not needed.

**K4. Option (b) depends on an access question nobody has answered.** "Warm introductions only" (§9, §13), but the CEO's 10-minute checks since C16 ("do you know a medical/regulatory writer, a clinical-operations person at a small biotech …") have no recorded answer (progress.md Open questions). If the answer is "no one", (b) cannot run without the cold contact the brief rules out. So (b) is only real if the CEO can name a person today.

**K5. Small inconsistencies in the (b) plan.** (i) §13 says "decide by 28 Feb 2027", but R4's kill date is **31 Mar 2027**; one of them is moot. (ii) §10 says €10k covers "3 packs × ~€2.3k" using the **employed** writer cost (€335–838 per pack), but the pilot uses a freelancer: at €82.2/h × 8–20 h the writer part is €658–1,644, so 3 four-language packs cost about €7.0–10.5k (*my math*), i.e. up to the whole personal budget. (iii) The C28 task description in `tasks.json` still shows the old recommendation "(b) … Wait for the critic review"; only its notes show (a). The CEO should read the notes, not the description.

**K6. Portfolio consequence of (a).** All other CEO decisions are closed (B3, C3, C5, C7, C9, C12, C16, C23 done; eldercare navigator parked, GLP-1 companion from the consumer route dropped). After (a), the spin-out route (kill date 28 Feb 2027) is the only live route, and C19 had named this trial variant as its fallback. *Inference:* if the spin-out kill line hits, the Sep 2027 full-time start has no candidate idea, and a new scan plus brief plus validation takes months. This does not argue for keeping the trial idea (it fails on its own numbers); it argues for the CEO deciding now whether a fresh scan starts before or after 28 Feb 2027.
