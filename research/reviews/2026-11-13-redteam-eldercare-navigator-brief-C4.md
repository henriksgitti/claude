## Fatal or near-fatal objections
None new against the brief as a document. The venture verdict stands. The points below are about how firmly the conclusion is held and what follows from it.

**N1. "K-a: Met" is absence of evidence on a soft threshold, not a measured kill.**
- Section 12: "No evidence of a **private** employer paying ≥€15,000 a year for eldercare support ... Status today: **Met**". Bottom line: "kill criterion K-a ... is met on today's desk evidence".
- The threshold is mine (first review, marked a judgment call). It comes from two numbers with nothing behind them: €1m ARR as the bar, and 60 customers, which section 5 says "has no funnel behind it". *My math:* at 100 customers the bar is €10,000; at 167 it is €5,988, the list price. The bar moves by a factor of 2.8 with the customer count.
- The price ceiling rests on a price nobody is shown to pay. The project's own K1 rule says "a price list counts only with a named paying customer". Eldercare24 has none, so by that rule it is not a price point at all: it shows neither a ceiling nor a floor. The private prices that are actually paid (famPlus, pme, heycare) are "not public"; K-b is "not checked beyond 'not public'".
- So the honest status is: **"no evidence found; incumbent prices not looked at"**, not "met". The direction is still right, because of N2 and because the only eldercare-only vendor at this price shows no customer. But the CEO should not read "Met" as a fact about the market.

**N2. The brief's own best evidence against the wedge is in its table and is not used: the bundle is worth about six times the eldercare lot.**
- Section 3, SWR row: lot 3 eldercare €84,034; lot 1 EAP €235,294; lot 2 family €184,873 (48 months, buyer's estimates).
- *My math:* all three lots = €504,201; / 4 = €126,050 a year; / 3,500 staff = **€36 per employee per year** (€24 if the 1,800 freelancers are covered). Eldercare alone was €4–6. Eldercare is 17 % of what this buyer expects to spend on employee support.
- Section 5 says: "**Size does not raise the price** ... No evidence item shows a price lever." There is one: scope. *My math:* at €24–36 per head a bundle passes the brief's own €15,000 bar at about 420–630 staff. One buyer's estimate, no award, same caveat as the €4–6.
- What this means:
  - The venture-scale route in this category exists and is the bundle. heycare shows it: founded 2022, about €4m raised, eight named customers on its homepage (fact-check C4, caveat 1), "says it is profitable".
  - That is the reason to drop wedge (ii), and it is stronger than K-a: **employers do pay, for the bundle, to vendors with a carer network and references**. A founder with €10,000, no network and no care qualification cannot build the bundle.
  - It also closes the door on "start with eldercare, add modules later": heycare did it the other way round, with childcare as the entry and capital behind it.
- **Buyer (HR lead, 600 staff):** "I buy one contract for childcare, crisis counselling and care. I will not run a second vendor, a second data-protection review and a second works-council round for the 17 % slice."

**N3. "The best case can pay three modest salaries" is a month-24 snapshot. The path to it is unfunded.**
- Bottom line: "the best case of 60 customers is €359,000 ARR. That can pay three modest salaries". Section 5 table: €180,000 a year, "nothing else".
- ARR at month 24 is not cash over 24 months. *My math, linear ramp from 0:* revenue collected over the 24 months ≈ €359,000 in the best case, ≈ €108,000 in the low case. Three people at €60,000 for 24 months cost €360,000. Gap: about €0 in the best case, about **€252,000 in the low case**, before the 3–4 months of launch and the 6-month pilot the brief plans ahead of the first converted contract, and before "sales, legal, insurance and tools".
- Money in sight per section 10: €10,000 own funds plus one grant of at most €36,000. EXIST and venture money are both called unlikely.
- So the sentence should read: even the best case needs three people to work up to two years for little or no pay. That answers the open half of F4: a qualified care adviser has no reason to take that deal over a salaried job at famPlus, pme or, from 2029, a Kasse.
- **Investor (Munich seed):** "You are not asking me for money because you know I will say no. Then who pays the adviser in month 3?"

**N4. The one step the brief keeps cannot change the L01 decision. Decide C3 now.**
- Bottom line: "Keep only the desk run on the care reform after the Bundestag vote". K-d: kill if "the PNOG passes with §§ 7a, 7c, 7d in substance"; "Open until the vote".
- Check both outcomes. Bill passes: K-d kills. Bill fails or is cut: K-a, K1 and K2 are unchanged, so the wedge is still dead. **K-d is redundant.** No result on 11 Dec reopens the employer wedge.
- What the run can find is a different company: tools or delivery for Kassen (signer cluster C5, "K2 unproven" for L03, start 2029, a bill, Nui already at AOK Bayern and DAK). The brief says so itself: "a different customer and a different company". It is a new long-list row, with weak founder fit (needs a care-management co-founder with Kasse access).
- Risk for a part-time CEO: C3 drifts for four weeks "until the vote". It should not. The run is cheap and can stay, filed as a Stage A check, not as a condition on L01.

**N5. Option (c) hands the decision to ideas that have not had this test. L01's fall is partly a selection effect.**

## Smaller caveats
**N6. One of the four "weak willingness to pay" items is weaker than written (my own argument in F1).** Section 2: "the offer rate moved 1.7 points" while five vendors sold. The 15.7 % counts firms with ≥5 staff. The bundlers sell to large employers (famPlus: "Über 550.000 Mitarbeiter*innen" covered). Coverage of employees could have grown a lot without moving a count dominated by small firms. So the item says little about firms with 50–1,000 staff, which is the brief's own "what stays open". The conclusion does not depend on it (N2 carries it).

**N7. Two kill rows would not work as written, if option (b) is ever chosen.**
- **K-g cannot tell a working product from a failing one.** "Fewer than 2 cases in total across the three pilot firms". *My math, Poisson:* at the expected 3.6 cases the pilot fails by chance 13 % of the time (P(≤1) = e^−3.6 × 4.6); at half the expected use (1.8) it still passes 54 % of the time. The brief concedes the pilot "cannot show the usage rate". Only the second half of K-g (renewal at list price) carries information, and section 12 admits renewal has no proof to rest on.
- **K-f is not a cheap test.** A paid pilot needs a delivering care adviser, the legal opinion (K-h, €2,000–4,000) and a company with insurance and data-protection set-up. Section 10 puts "capital to first revenue" at €8,000–15,000, "at or above the €10,000 own budget". The test that would confirm the idea costs the whole budget and needs the co-founder first. A cheaper substitute is under "Cheapest tests".
- A cancellable €250 a month is a €1,500 commitment. It says little about a 24-month contract at €299–499.

**N8. Wedge (i) disappeared without a test.** Section 1 drops tooling for the bundlers on "no evidence that any bundler buys third-party tooling"; section 12 offers no option for it. It is the only wedge that uses the founder's actual skill (AI agent implementation). With 5–10 buyers it is not a venture either, so this does not change the verdict. But "not found on vendor pages" is a thin reason to close it; one conversation would settle it.

**N9. Carried over, unchanged:** no survivorship search; usage rate is a guess; PNOG is a bill; vendor customer counts are claims; all thresholds (€15,000, 9 of 15, 2 of 8, 2 cases) are judgment calls.
